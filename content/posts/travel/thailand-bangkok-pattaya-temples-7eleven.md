# Bangkok and Pattaya: Temples, 7-Elevens, and Questionable Decisions

If I had to choose one country to keep going back to, **Thailand would definitely be it**.

The convenience of **7-Elevens every five steps**, the food, the beaches, the massage places, the relative ease of travel, and yes - the fact that you can go from a Michelin-recognized meal to something grilled on the street that may or may not change your life.

And of course, legal marijuana.

Thailand just works.

I broadly think of Thailand in **three parts**:

- **Central Thailand** - **Bangkok** and **Pattaya**
- **Southern Thailand** - **Phuket, Krabi, and the Phi Phi Islands**  
  *[Read this post for the south.](./post.html?section=travel&slug=thailand-south-placeholder)*
- **Northern Thailand** - **Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai, and Pai**  
  *[Read this post for the north.](./post.html?section=travel&slug=thailand-north-placeholder)*

This post is about the **central belt**: **Bangkok and Pattaya**.

## I. Bangkok: do the classic nonsense once

If you are a first-timer in Bangkok, there are a bunch of things you should just **tick off once and for all**.

After that, Bangkok becomes the kind of place you return to for food, flights, shopping, and chaos - not because you urgently need to see every temple again in 37-degree humidity.

If you are backpacking, **stay around Khao San Road**. It is loud, messy, touristy, convenient, and exactly what it should be.

## II. Things to do in Bangkok

### 1. Do the temple-and-palace starter pack

Just accept your fate and do the obvious things once:

- **The Grand Palace**
- **Wat Phra Kaew**
- **Wat Arun**
- **Wat Pho**

This is Bangkok's official "yes yes, I have done culture" package.

You will sweat, you will take photos, and you will emerge spiritually improved or at least dehydrated.

Also, do not fall for the classic tuk-tuk detour line saying "the palace is closed today" and offering to take you elsewhere. It is almost always a scam loop.

### 2. Ancient City and Erawan Museum

These are actually worth it.

The **Ancient City** is huge, so please do not lie to yourself about walking the whole place. **Take the golf cart** and preserve both your knees and your will to live.

Pair it with the **Erawan Museum**, which is dramatic, strange, and very photogenic in the way Thailand often is.

### 3. Chatuchak Market

If you enjoy shopping, browsing, bargaining, mild confusion, and buying things you absolutely did not need, go to **Chatuchak**.

You can find almost anything here:
- clothes
- souvenirs
- decor
- random nonsense
- even more random nonsense

It is one of those markets where you will either feel extremely alive or mildly trapped. Usually both.

### 4. IconSiam and Asiatique

These are less "essential Bangkok" and more **pleasantly polished urban consumerism**.

Good for:
- window shopping
- air-conditioning
- pretending you are richer than you are
- waterfront vibes

### 5. Platinum Fashion Mall and MBK

If your tastes lean more toward **souvenirs, cheap finds, and mildly shameless shopping**, then **Platinum** and **MBK** are better bets.

This is where Bangkok gets practical.

### 6. Terminal 21

**Terminal 21** is one of the few malls that is actually fun as an experience.

Each floor is themed like a different city, which sounds gimmicky - and is - but in a way that works. It is much more entertaining than a mall has any business being.

### 7. Lumphini Park

If you wake up early voluntarily, first of all, why.

But if you do, **Lumphini Park** is a nice calmer side of Bangkok. Good for a morning walk before the city fully wakes up and starts throwing traffic and bad decisions at you.

### 8. Feeling naughty?

Well.

There is always **Soi Cowboy** or **Nana** at night, depending on how curious, bored, reckless, or anthropologically interested you are feeling.

If you are in the Soi Cowboy or Nana area, carry limited cash and avoid keeping open tabs all night.

I am not here to judge.
I am here to document.

## III. What to eat in Bangkok

There are plenty of great restaurants in Bangkok, and I have not explored nearly enough of them to act like a food authority.

But honestly, **street food is the point**.

A few things worth calling out:

- **Thipsamai** for **Pad Thai** - still my favorite Pad Thai, and yes, it has also been featured in the Michelin Guide
- **Kolun.h** for Hainanese noodles and mouth-watering crispy pork belly
- **Ready-to-eat food from 7-Eleven** - which sounds ridiculous until you are in Thailand and realize 7-Eleven may be the greatest institution in the country after Buddhism

I have not explored enough of Bangkok's restaurant scene to give you a holy list. But if you eat aggressively, stay curious, and never disrespect street food, you will do just fine.

## IV. Pattaya: less depth, more spectacle

Let me be honest: **there is not a lot to do in Pattaya** in the meaningful, layered, culturally nourishing sense.

But that does not mean there is nothing.

### 1. Getting there

You can book buses to Pattaya on **12Go**, which is the clean and civilized option.

Or, if you are on a budget and do not mind waiting for the next bus with seats, you can just go to the **bus terminus** and usually pay **around half the fare**.

Thailand often gives you these choices:
- pay more, plan less
- pay less, stand around a bit

### 2. Things to do in Pattaya

#### Sanctuary of Truth

This is the main thing.

The **Sanctuary of Truth** is the one place in Pattaya that genuinely stands out. It is grand, detailed, dramatic, and so much more beautiful than the rest of Pattaya prepares you for.

This is not optional. Go.

#### Walking Street

Of course.

You cannot come to Pattaya and then pretend **Walking Street** does not exist. Whether you enjoy it, judge it, laugh at it, or observe it like a live sociology experiment is your personal journey.

But yes, go once.

#### Alcazar Cabaret

Catch a show at **Alcazar Cabaret**. Easy, fun, polished, and deeply Thailand in a way that is hard to explain but easy to enjoy.

#### Nong Nooch Garden

If you want something more landscaped and less neon, **Nong Nooch Garden** exists to provide that contrast.

## V. Final thought

Bangkok and Pattaya are probably not the parts of Thailand that will own your heart forever.

That is more likely to happen in the islands or in the north.

But they are very good entry points.

Bangkok gives you temples, food, markets, malls, nightlife, and enough chaos to keep you alert. Pattaya gives you spectacle, a few very specific attractions, and a reminder that Thailand contains multitudes.

Do the classics once. Eat without fear. Use 7-Eleven shamelessly. Make a few questionable choices. Then move on to the rest of the country.

Thailand gets better from there.
