Beer, beaches, and bad decisions.

Goa holds a special place in my heart. I spent most of my undergrad days staring at its beautiful skies (unfortunately, COVID stole a year and a half). I try to visit Goa at least once as a pilgrimage with my college buddies.

Goa is one of the smaller states in India, but the number of things you can do here is immense. There are great beaches, amazing food, and a culture that caters to everyone. Along with the Portuguese churches, the temples here are beautiful too.

There is North Goa, which is famous for its nightlife, with streets bustling with tourists and locals alike.
And there is South Goa (about a 2-hour drive from the north), with fewer crowds and a serene, laid-back experience.

If you are flying to Goa, you can land either at Dabolim (GOI), which is almost equidistant from North and South Goa, or at the newer Mopa (GOX), which is in North Goa. Choose accordingly, depending on where you plan to stay.

## I. North Goa

1. Panjim - the capital city of Goa.
  - Fontainhas - Portuguese colony, a good photo stop.
  - Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception Church.
  - Dona Paula.
  - Aguada Fort.
  - Miramar Beach.
  - Maruti Temple.

2. Beaches and Beers

  - Calangute - go for water activities; otherwise, it feels like any other beach.
  - Vagator Beach - lovely spot.
    - Chapora Fort overlooks the beach, so plan one sunset from here. One of the iconic scenes from the Bollywood movie Dil Chahta Hai was shot here.
  - Anjuna Beach - beach plus rocks, so it feels different. Do not miss dining at Claudi's, preferably at night with a beer on the beach.
  - Baga Beach for nightlife.
    - Tito's Lane - a small walking street with pubs, shops, and restaurants.
  - For a different kind of nightlife in North Goa, try the floating casinos on the Mandovi River near Panjim (Deltin and Casino Pride are popular picks).
  - A bit farther north, with fewer crowds and cleaner beaches: Morjim, Ashwem, Mandrem, Arambol, and Querim.

3. Food
  - Bomras - for excellent Burmese cuisine (their tuna dish is my favorite).
  - Petisco, Panjim - great food and a nice cocktail menu.
  - Anand Sea Food - amazing local cuisine and fish thalis.
  - Artjuna Cafe - I am a sucker for their Croissant Salmon Eggs Benedict and tiramisu. My friends go crazy for the pistachio croissant.
  - Ritz Classic - a true classic and a great Goan cuisine restaurant.

## II. Old Goa / Velha Goa

1. Churches and Museum Circuit - this old part of Goa has some of the most beautiful churches in Goa.

  - This can be covered while heading to South Goa in around two to four hours.
  - Cover these in this order to optimize travel:
    - Ruins of St. Augustine
    - Museum of Christian Art (right opposite the previous one)
    - Basilica of Bom Jesus - this is the most famous one because it is a World Heritage Site with a first-class relic. (Relic classes range from physical bodily remains (first class), to items owned by the saint (second class), to objects touched to those items (third class).) So be ready for a surprise.
    - Se Cathedral and Archaeological Museum (across the road)
    - St. Cajetan Church (my personal favorite)
  - Make sure you check opening and closing times; they vary.

## III. South Goa

1. Places to Cover

  - Cavelossim Beach - a peaceful white sand beach.
  - Cola Beach - my favorite beach in all of Goa. You need to walk a bit from the mainland, but it is totally worth it. It also has backwaters for kayaking.
  - Butterfly Beach - you need to do a short hike, but there is a lovely rocky crescent beach at the end.
  - Palolem Beach - one of the few happening beaches in South Goa.
  - Cabo de Rama Fort - you can catch beautiful sunsets here.
  - Chapoli Falls.
  - Shri Shantadurga Temple

## IV. Things to Eat and Drink

1. Goa Food Hits
  - Seafood.
    - Calamari (squid), shrimp and prawns, crab, fish, snails (foodies only)
  - Pork Vindaloo
  - Chicken Xacuti
  - Ros Omelette
  - Serradura and Bebinca - really good Goan desserts. I would die for serradura. I would rather die eating serradura or tiramisu than anything else.
  - Cashew Feni - a local alcoholic drink.
  - Port wine - a Goa classic worth trying.
  - People's beer - my favourtie bottled beer!

## V. My Two Cents

1. Final Notes

  - Do not miss a sunset. No two sunsets are the same.
  - There are plenty of places in Goa. Even a year is not enough to cover everything. In the rush to cover all spots, do not miss living in the moment. It is okay to miss a few places. There is always a next time.
  - Be careful while riding bikes. Wear helmets.

If you are wondering why Bogmalo Beach (the one in the first picture) is not mentioned, it is because, although beautiful, it is in central Goa, and going there just for that may not be worth it. It is right next to Dabolim Airport, so if you have time before your flight, definitely check it out. Also, Claudi's Corner here is a nice restaurant. This beach is the closest one to our campus, so we used to come here almost every weekend. That is why this beach holds a special place in my heart...
