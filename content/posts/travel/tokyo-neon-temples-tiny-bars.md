# Toooookyoooooo: Neon, Temples, Tiny Bars, and Too Much to Do

"There are cities, there are metropolises, and then there is Tokyo."

![Tokyo skyline view with Mount Fuji in the distance](assets/images/posts/travel/tokyo-tile-skyline.png "Tokyo skyline with Mount Fuji in the backdrop | landscape")

Tokyo is one of those cities that feels impossibly large until you realize it is actually a collection of very distinct neighborhoods, each with its own personality, pace, and obsession.

One minute you are in a quiet temple district at sunrise, the next you are surrounded by anime, kitchen knives, capsule toys, and twelve floors of things you did not know you wanted. Then somehow, by night, you are bar-hopping through alleys so narrow they feel fictional.

Tokyo can be overwhelming if you try to do everything. The trick is not to conquer it. The trick is to move through it in clusters.

## I. Where to stay

If nightlife is the priority, stay around **Shinjuku** or **Shibuya**. These are the easiest bases if you want late nights, bright lights, food, bars, shopping, and the general feeling that the city refuses to sleep.

If you care more about an early, calmer start at **Sensoji Temple**, stay around **Taito** or **Asakusa**, especially near the river. That part of Tokyo gives you easier access to historical Tokyo and a much softer morning atmosphere.

## II. First Tokyo rule: stay close to the Yamanote Line

If you are trying to navigate Tokyo efficiently, stay close to the **Yamanote Line** - the green loop line that connects most of the major centers of the city.

Also, just get an **pasmo** or equivalent transit card and stop thinking too hard about tickets.

## III. Things to do in Tokyo

Tokyo works best in clusters. Instead of trying to cover the city, move through neighborhoods that naturally fit together.

### 1. Start with sushi for breakfast

If possible, begin with a **fish market breakfast** - **Toyosu** or **Tsukiji** depending on what kind of experience you want. Fresh sushi for breakfast feels like one of those very Tokyo things that sounds excessive until you actually do it.

### 2. Do one immersive digital-art stop

Pick one of the **teamLab** experiences - **Planets** or **Borderless**. You probably do not need both on a short trip, so just choose one and do it properly.

### 4. Walk the Meiji Jingu -> Harajuku -> Omotesando -> Shibuya stretch

This is one of the best sequences in Tokyo.

Start at **Meiji Jingu Shrine**, inside the broader **Yoyogi Park** area, then cross into **Harajuku**, walk through **Takeshita Dori**, continue into **Omotesando**, and end in **Shibuya**.

This one stretch gives you:
- shrine calm
- teenage chaos
- street fashion
- polished architecture
- major-city energy

### 5. Get a free skyline view from the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building

If you are around Shinjuku, go up to the **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Office observation deck**. It is a very easy, very satisfying city view without the emotional damage of yet another ticket.

### 6. Shop for collectibles at Nakano Broadway

If you like anime, figures, manga, retro finds, or hobby-store chaos, **Nakano Broadway** is one of the best places to browse. It is especially good if Akihabara feels too obvious and you want something that still feels deeply Tokyo.

### 7. Explore Akihabara for anime, electronics, and books

**Akihabara** is still essential if you enjoy anime culture, gaming, electronics, or niche hobby shopping. It can be overstimulating in the best and worst ways.

Good stop here:
- **Shosen Books**

### 8. Spend an early morning in Asakusa

**Sensoji Temple** is best seen early, before the crowds really build.

Walk through **Nakamise Street**, then explore the area around it slowly. If you are hungry afterward, **Hoppy Street** is a good place to keep in mind.

### 10. Go to Kappabashi if you like kitchen gear

If you like kitchen tools, dining ware, knives, or niche specialty shopping, go to **Kappabashi Street**.

For knives specifically, note:
- **Kappabashi Hondori**

### 11. Visit Tokyo National Museum and Ueno Park

If you want a more traditional museum-and-park Tokyo day, **Tokyo National Museum** and **Ueno Park** are a strong pairing. This is a good counterweight to all the shopping-heavy districts.

### 12. Leave room for the Imperial Palace area

If time allows, you can also do the **Imperial Gardens / Imperial Palace area**. It is not the most overwhelming stop in Tokyo, but it works well if you want something quieter and more spacious in between denser neighborhoods.

### 13. Do Tokyo shopping in themes

Tokyo rewards highly specific shopping interests. Depending on what you like, these are all worth considering:

- **C-Pla in Shibuya** for gachapon and tiny toy chaos
- **Meyoko** for souvenirs and general shopping
- **Tokyo Station Underground Mall**, including the **Tomica Shop**
- **Traveller's Factory** for stationery and notebooks
- **Nippori** for fabric
- **Ochanomizu** for music stores

### 14. Keep Shinjuku for the night

Shinjuku is best after dark.

Walk through:
- **Kabukicho** for neon and visual chaos
- **Omoide Yokocho** for tiny alleyway eateries
- **Golden Gai** for its dense cluster of tiny bars

A good way to do the night:
- start with food in **Omoide Yokocho**
- continue into **Golden Gai**
- then let the night decide the rest

Beer and starters at **Omoide Yokocho** is a perfect way to begin the night.

## IV. Final thought

Tokyo is not a city you finish. It is a city you sample.

You get temples, toy stores, shrines, records, anime, fabric, knives, fish markets, observation decks, tiny bars, and vending machines that seem more emotionally available than some people.

It is loud, disciplined, absurdly efficient, and deeply specific.

And the best way to do it is not to chase everything.

Pick a line. Pick a cluster. Walk a lot. Stay out late. Wake up early. Repeat.
