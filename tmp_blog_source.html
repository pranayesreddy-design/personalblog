The gold mines in supply chains

- September 14, 2022

 




Last week, I went to my alma matter one last time as a student for my convocation. A very clean and ironed satin gown, scarf and a cap make up the attire of the 1000 students who receive the degrees with pride. We had to pay a refundable security deposit of INR1000/$12.5 to receive the attire and you return it to the admin after the ceremony to claim your deposit. 

You take hundreds of pictures for a life time in the attire with people who you have spent about 20% of your life until that point. But what do you do with the satin gown that's useless for you once the day is over? You probably might never wear it again and let it rot in some corner of your attic. Or you could return it to the admin and get back your money, unless you want to keep it as a souvenir.

But where did these hundreds of gowns come form? The admin probably might have bought them long back and recycle the same gowns every year. That could be an option, but it involves a lot of space and effort for the inventory and maintenance respectively. Or, the admin could have rented them out for the day from some vendor. 

Apparently, I've learnt that there are only two big rental vendors for the satin gowns in the entire India! (or probably the entire sub-continent). They take care of the maintenance and rent thousands of gowns out to thousands of colleges (like ours) across the sub-continent - every year!! Thinking about the scale, "If you look at India, approximately 65 lakh graduates pass out every year" - The Hans India. Quite a huge market! 

You know what's more interesting? The market and tradition has been there for the satin gowns since the colonial era and the same vendor who provided the gowns for us has also provided a gown to Smt. Indira Gandhi - the former Prime Minister of India 50 years ago. 

Every thing we use everyday has a deep supply chain, and a gold mine to be mined if looked closely. Look at things around you and trace back to the source and you will surprise yourself form the things you get to know. Ball bearings are present almost in any rotating part around you - fans, cars etc. Those bearings probably might have manufactured by SKF India. The Incense sticks are made from incense powder. And there are only a handful companies that manufacture it. 

You don't have to build for the next big thing to create wealth. There is wealth that can be created in the supply chain of the next big thing. And there is plenty of gold to be mined. Anywhere. 

Seek, and ye shall find.
